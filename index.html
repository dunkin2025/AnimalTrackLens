THE PROJECT

Let computers handle the hours of video.

Wildlife cameras, zoo webcams, and research cameras can produce enormous amounts of footage. The goal is to help researchers find and quantify meaningful observations without manually reviewing every frame.

◉

Animal Detection

Locate animals in video and identify the species when the model supports it.

↝

Tracking

Follow animals across frames and measure movement, location, and interactions.

⌁

Behavior Detection

Classify observable activities such as eating, walking, resting, and social interaction.

▥

Behavior Analysis

Convert observations into timelines and structured data for research and review.

THE PIPELINE

Video in. Behavioral data out.

The system is designed as a sequence of measurable stages rather than treating animal behavior as a single black-box prediction.

01VIDEOCamera or authorized footage
→
02DETECTIONFind animals in frames
→
03TRACKINGFollow individuals over time
→
04BEHAVIORClassify observable actions
→
05DATATimeline and research output

BEHAVIOR CATEGORIES

Start with behaviors we can observe.

Behavior definitions should be species-specific and scientifically validated. The project can begin with simple, visually distinguishable behaviors and expand as better datasets become available.

WalkingRunningEatingDrinking RestingSleepingGroomingPlaying Social interactionChasingAggressionNesting SwimmingClimbingOther / uncertain

RESEARCH & VALIDATION

AI predictions need human verification.

The project is not intended to replace zoologists or animal behavior researchers. Human annotations and expert review are essential for defining behaviors, training models, and evaluating results.

AccuracyHow often classifications are correct
PrecisionHow reliable positive detections are
RecallHow many relevant events are detected
F1 scoreBalance between precision and recall
